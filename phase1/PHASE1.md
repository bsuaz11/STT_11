1. how you track state
    The way our bot keeps track of what it owns and whether its winning our losing is by updating its records only when the exchange says a trade actually happened which is the role of the on_fill callback. The bot does not use the orders that it sends as a way to keep track of state because it's possible that the order is not filled. As for what the bot actually records per stock, it records how many shares we hold, the average price we paid, and also how much profit we're achieving by selling. In addition to the shares that I buy from sending orders, there are free shares like IPO shares, the ones at open, or the trades that happened while I was disconnected. To account for this, the bot compares its own count of shares to the exchange's count evry 5 seconds. If it so happens that the counts disagree twice in a row, it replaces its own count with the exchange's count and logs a RECON line that tells the user that the shares count for the bot were reconiliated due to discrepancy. This way, I can make sure that my records don't drift. Additionally, our code also prints a STATUS line which contains our P&L next to the exchange's to prove that they match. Lastly, our bot avoids rejections by never selling shars that it doesnt own, never sending a new order until the last one has settled, and sending nothing during pre-open since the exchange will just reject it then.

2. how you reconnect
    The way that our bot reconnects after a disconnect is that it has a base delay starting at 1 second and then tries again to connect after that. If it still fails, then the bot will wait twice as long (i.e. 1,2,4,8) until up to 30 seconds. We handled the disconnect this way because we don't want our bot to keep trying to connect to a server that is down. After the bot reconnects, the old order book data is out of date so it gets thrown away and the bot waits for the first new snapshot from the exchange and then pulls cash and positions from the leaderboard. The bot also forgets about ordeers it had in queue since we don't know what happened to them.

3. your IPO decision with the reasoning.
    Our IPO decision is to bid at the top of the range of shares for a size where full allocation doesn't exceed 10% of our cash. We chose to bid at the top because it makes the allocation certain in the sense that bidding at the high range guarantees we get some of whatever price the stock clears at. However, we choose to keep te size small because if we inflate our order too much to beat the scale=down, then we would be holding too many shares when demand is weak. We'd prefer to miss some of the profit from the listing day rather than have too many shares of the stock.
